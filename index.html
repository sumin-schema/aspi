The world's most consequential political gathering

Held every five years in the Great Hall of the People, the National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party is the world's most consequential political gathering. It installs new leadership, sets ideological direction, and signals China's priorities to the rest of the world for the half-decade ahead.

The 21st Congress, expected in late 2027, arrives at an unusual moment: an aging leadership cohort, a slowing economy, and a General Secretary who has dismantled the norms that once governed transitions. For the first time since the Mao era, there is no established mechanism for what comes after Xi Jinping — and no obvious answer to the question of who rules China next.

This guide maps the current power structure, traces the road to 2027, and identifies the personnel and institutional signals that will define China's political trajectory for a generation.

The stakes

Why 2027 matters more than most

Every five years, China's National Congress resets the top of the party. But the 21st Congress is unusually consequential: it arrives as Xi Jinping — having abolished presidential term limits in 2018 — seeks a fourth consecutive term as General Secretary, an arrangement without modern precedent.

Observers will scrutinize the new Politburo Standing Committee lineup for clues about succession. If no obvious heir is elevated to the PSC's second-ranked position, it signals Xi intends to rule indefinitely. If a younger figure is elevated and positioned, it could mark the beginning of a transition — however distant.

"The 21st Congress will reveal whether Xi has solved the dictator's dilemma — or deferred it." — PRCLeader.org
The economic backdrop

15th Five-Year Plan shapes the agenda

The congress will follow China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), which emphasizes "high-quality development" — a shift away from GDP-at-all-costs toward domestic consumption, technological self-reliance, and managed slowing of growth. The plan responds to structural headwinds: an aging population, a property sector still recovering from crisis, and sustained US–China technology decoupling.

The new Standing Committee will inherit these challenges and be judged by how they navigate them. Personnel choices at the congress will signal which factions and economic philosophies hold sway in the years ahead.

Key watch: Will the incoming Premier be a technocrat or a loyalist? The answer will signal how much Xi trusts economic expertise over political fidelity.

Intelligence brief

Four things to watch at the 21st Congress

01
Is there a designated successor?

Under Deng Xiaoping's norm, a heir-apparent was elevated to PSC rank 2 with a decade to prepare. Xi broke this pattern in 2022. If a figure aged under 60 is elevated to rank 2 in 2027, succession is back on the table. If not, the implied message is indefinite rule.

02
Who controls the economy?

The incoming Premier (rank 2 PSC) and the Vice Premiers will signal whether Xi trusts technocratic expertise or prefers political reliability. The appointment of a market-oriented economist would reassure foreign investors; a pure loyalist would reinforce statist tendencies.

03
Military leadership reshuffled?

The Central Military Commission will also be restaffed. Following the 2023 dismissals of senior PLA Rocket Force commanders over corruption, the military reshuffle carries unusual weight. Watch for how many Xi-era generals survive, and whether the CMC vice chairs change.

04
Age-norm exceptions

Convention holds that officials over 68 retire at congresses. Xi broke this for himself in 2022. Will he extend similar exceptions to allies like Wang Huning (72 in 2027)? Exceptions signal a deliberate dismantling of institutionalised succession — or a tight inner circle held together by personal trust.
